What I do

You don't have a tool problem.

You already own the sequencer, the dialer, and the data provider. What's missing is someone who picks up the phone every morning and writes the email that actually gets answered. That's the whole job.

  1. 01

    Cold calls, every day

    Real dials into real gatekeepers. Not a "call block" that gets moved. A four-minute conversation surfaces timing and budget that no email sequence ever will.

  2. 02

    Email written per account

    Domains, inboxes, deliverability, and copy. If a message could have been sent to a thousand companies without changing a word, it doesn't go out.

  3. 03

    Qualifying, out loud

    Problem, timing, budget, and who else signs — confirmed on the call, not guessed from a form fill. Half the value here is the meetings I don't book.

  4. 04

    A handoff that sticks

    Your rep opens the invite already knowing the story: why they took it, what they're fixing, who else is in the room. No cold restarts.

The short version

What I won't do.

  • Buy you a scraped list
  • Report open rates as progress
  • Book a junk meeting to hit a number
  • Pretend this works in month one

Outbound compounds. The first month is list, messaging, and finding which objection actually stops the conversation. Month two is when the calendar starts filling.

Process

Five steps. Same order, every account.

None of it is proprietary. It's just done consistently — which is the part nobody can sustain while also closing deals.

  1. 01

    Agree on who counts

    Titles, company size, trigger events. We settle what "qualified" means before I make a single dial, so nobody argues about lead quality in week three.

  2. 02

    Build the list by hand

    Account by account, verified. Bulk lists burn your sending domain and waste the calls.

  3. 03

    Run both channels at once

    Sequences run against the same accounts I'm dialing. Whichever one they answer, there's a person on the other end who knows their business.

  4. 04

    Qualify live

    If it doesn't hold up on the phone, it doesn't reach your calendar.

  5. 05

    Hand it off clean

    Meeting booked with notes attached. Weekly numbers on dials, replies, meetings set, and meetings actually held — that last one is the only metric that counts.

Fit

I'd rather tell you no now.

Worth a call

  • You sell B2B at a deal size that justifies a human conversation.
  • You have paying customers but no consistent outbound.
  • Your closers are good and simply short on meetings.
  • You need pipeline this quarter, not a hiring process.
  • You can give me 30 minutes a week of feedback.

Don't bother

  • You want a list to blast yourself.
  • You're selling B2C or low-ticket transactional.
  • Volume matters to you more than fit.
  • Nobody internally can take a meeting within two weeks.
  • You want leads with no input on who they are.

Tell me who you sell to.
