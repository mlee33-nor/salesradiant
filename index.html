What I do

You don't have a tool problem.

You already own the sequencer, the dialer, and the data provider. What's missing is someone who picks up the phone every morning and writes the email that actually gets answered. That's the whole job.

  1. 01

    Cold calls, every day

    Real dials into real gatekeepers — not a "call block" that keeps getting moved. Four minutes on the phone surfaces timing and budget that no email sequence ever will.

  2. 02

    Email written per account

    Domains, inboxes, deliverability, and copy. If a message could go to a thousand companies without changing a word, it doesn't go out.

  3. 03

    Qualifying, out loud

    Problem, timing, budget, and who else signs — confirmed on the call, not guessed from a form fill. Half the value here is the meetings I don't book.

  4. 04

    A handoff that sticks

    Your rep opens the invite already knowing the story: why they took it, what they're fixing, who else is in the room. No cold restarts.

What I won't do.

  • Pretend this works in month one

Outbound compounds. Month one is list, messaging, and finding which objection actually stops the conversation. Month two is when the calendar fills.

Process

Five steps. Same order, every account.

None of it is proprietary. It's just done consistently — which is the part nobody can sustain while also closing deals.

  1. 01

    Agree on who counts

    Titles, company size, trigger events. We settle what "qualified" means before I make a single dial, so nobody argues about lead quality in week three.

  2. 02

    Build the list by hand

    Account by account, verified. Bulk lists burn your sending domain and waste the calls.

  3. 03

    Run both channels at once

    Sequences run against the same accounts I'm dialing. Whichever one they answer, there's a person on the other end who knows their business.

  4. 04

    Qualify live

    If it doesn't hold up on the phone, it doesn't reach your calendar.

  5. 05

    Hand it off clean

    Meeting booked with notes attached. Weekly numbers on dials, replies, meetings set, and meetings actually held — that last one is the only metric that counts.

Fit

I'd rather tell you no now.

Worth a call

  • You sell B2B at a deal size that justifies a human conversation.
  • You have paying customers but no consistent outbound.
  • Your closers are good and simply short on meetings.
  • You need pipeline this quarter, not a hiring process.
  • You can give me 30 minutes a week of feedback.

Don't bother

  • You want a list to blast yourself.
  • You're selling B2C or low-ticket transactional.
  • Volume matters to you more than fit.
  • Nobody internally can take a meeting within two weeks.
  • You want leads with no input on who they are.
Questions

The ones I get on every first call.

How fast do meetings start landing?
Usually week three or four. Weeks one and two are list build, domain warm-up, and finding the message that gets a reply. Anyone promising you meetings in week one is either sitting on a bought list or planning to book anybody who picks up.
Do you use my domain or your own?
Mine. I set up separate sending domains that redirect to your site, so if deliverability takes a hit during testing it never touches your primary domain or your team's day-to-day email.
What happens when a meeting no-shows?
I re-book it. No-shows get counted separately from meetings held in the weekly report, because held is the only number that means anything — and hiding it would make the reporting useless to both of us.
Can you work a list I already have?
Yes, and I'll tell you honestly what shape it's in first. Most in-house lists are half stale. I'd rather verify and rebuild the weak half than burn a sending domain proving a point.
Do I need a CRM already set up?
Helpful, not required. If you have one I'll work inside it so your reps see the full history. If you don't, you'll get a clean handoff doc per meeting and we can talk about whether a CRM is even worth it yet.
How does pricing work?
A flat monthly retainer for the outbound work, agreed up front against a target meeting volume — no per-lead pricing, because paying per lead is exactly what pushes an agency to book junk. Exact numbers depend on the vertical and how hard the buyer is to reach; we'll cover it on the call.

Tell me who you sell to.

Twenty minutes, no deck. Your ICP, what outbound you've already tried, and what a real meeting is worth to you. If it's not a fit, you'll know before we hang up.
